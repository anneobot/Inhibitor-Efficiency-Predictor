# Models

Every configuration trained, its settings, and what it was for.

Fifteen configurations across five families. Every model received the same 283-column
descriptor-and-condition matrix, or its graph or sequence equivalent carrying the **identical**
72-column condition block, and every model was evaluated on the same fold indices under all
three partitioning schemes. The architectural rationale is in [`METHODS.md`](METHODS.md) §3;
this document is the inventory.

## 2. Detail by family

### 2.1 Classical

**Ridge** provides the linear reference against which any non-linear gain is measured. Its
failure is informative: it is the worst model under grouping (0.256) and actively harmful
under scaffolding (**−0.407**, worse than predicting the training-fold mean). Corrosion
inhibition is non-linear in both concentration and temperature, and a model assuming linearity
in the untransformed variables is misspecified from the outset.

**Random forest** (300 trees, unrestricted depth) and **gradient boosting** (300 stages) give
two tree ensembles with different bias–variance characteristics.

**XGBoost** at 500 rounds, learning rate 0.05, maximum depth 7, with row and column subsampling
at 0.85.

**SVR** with an RBF kernel, penalty 10.0 and scaled kernel coefficient, as a kernel method for
contrast.

Tree ensembles were the primary candidates because the input is a heterogeneous tabular matrix
of 283 columns mixing continuous physical quantities, logarithmic transforms, binary
indicators and high-cardinality categorical encodings across 8,359 rows. Tree ensembles are
close to optimal in that regime and handle the interactions among those parts without the
practitioner specifying interaction terms in advance.

**Structure-only controls.** A random forest and an XGBoost given the descriptor matrix
without the condition block. These exist to isolate the contribution of the experimental
conditions with the estimator, the folds and the metric all held constant, so that any
difference is attributable to the information supplied and not to the learning algorithm. They
are the controls behind the central ablation result.

### 2.2 Feedforward network

Input → 256 (BatchNorm, ReLU, dropout 0.15) → 128 (same) → 128 → 1 linear.
AdamW, learning rate 1 × 10⁻³, weight decay 1 × 10⁻⁴, batch 128, up to 150 epochs, early
stopping on validation loss at patience 15.

Its behaviour across schemes is the clearest single illustration of why the partitioning
matters: **fifth place row-wise at 0.764, eleventh when grouped at 0.303** — a loss of 0.461,
the largest of any model. Networks that can memorise a molecule from its descriptor vector
lose the most when memorisation stops paying.

### 2.3 Graph networks

**MPNN** in the formulation of Gilmer et al. (2017), implemented with edge-conditioned
convolution. A linear layer projects the eighteen node features to hidden width 96; three
message-passing steps update each node from its neighbours through a bond-conditioned weight
matrix generated by a small network from the three edge features; node states are pooled to a
graph-level vector by concatenating mean and sum pooling.

**GAT** (Veličković et al., 2018) with two attention layers, the first using four heads at
hidden width 96 and the second collapsing to a single head, dropout 0.15 inside the attention
mechanism.

**Condition fusion, in both.** The graph-level vector is concatenated with a 64-dimensional
embedding obtained by passing the 72-dimensional condition vector through a two-layer network,
before the joint representation enters the regression head. Both trained with AdamW at
1 × 10⁻³, weight decay 1 × 10⁻⁵, batch 64, up to 120 epochs, early stopping at patience 15.

This fusion corrects a defect in the earlier pipeline, where the graph networks received
structure alone while the classical models additionally received the conditions. Under that
arrangement their weak performance measured the handicap, not the architecture.

**Even corrected, they do not lead**, and they degrade sharply under scaffolding — the MPNN
falls from 0.335 grouped to **0.057** scaffold, the GAT from 0.362 to 0.109. Competitive on a
new molecule, near-useless on a new chemical framework.

### 2.4 Hybrids

Constructed in direct response to methodological review, which raised the absence of any
hybridised approach as a gap. Each couples a learned molecular representation to a tabular
learner, on the reasoning that structure and conditions have different statistical character
and may be better served by different machinery.

| | Representation branch | Regressor | Grouped R² |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H1** | MPNN encoder, trained on the fold then **frozen**, graph-level embedding extracted | XGBoost, 400 rounds | **0.451** |
| **H2** | ChemBERTa, frozen, 384-d sequence embedding computed **once per distinct structure** | XGBoost, 400 rounds | 0.411 |
| **H3** | sequence ⊕ graph ⊕ tabular branches trained **jointly**, concatenated at 128/128/64 | shared head, AdamW with plateau scheduler (×0.5, patience 6) | 0.337 |

**H1 is the clearest positive result among the deep approaches.** It reaches 0.451 grouped,
statistically indistinguishable from XGBoost and the random forest, by letting the graph
network do representation learning and the boosted ensemble do the regression. It does not
transfer: 0.247 under scaffolding against the tree ensembles' 0.398.

H2 computes embeddings once per molecule rather than once per row — the embedding depends only
on the structure, so computing it 8,359 times instead of 1,050 would be waste.

**H3, the most elaborate configuration in the study, performs worst of the three** (0.337).
End-to-end joint training of three branches on 8,359 rows is not supported by this data
volume.

### 2.5 Chemistry transformer

ChemBERTa `77M-MTR` — a RoBERTa architecture of three transformer layers at hidden width 384,
pretrained by multi-task regression over a large corpus of molecular structures — loaded as a
bare encoder with its pretraining regression head discarded.

The head used here pools token representations by mask-weighted mean, concatenates a
64-dimensional projection of the condition block, and passes the result through a two-layer
head to a scalar. Tokenisation uses the model's own tokeniser at a maximum of 128 tokens.

**Discriminative learning rate.** The pretrained encoder updates at 3 × 10⁻⁵ while the
randomly initialised condition branch and head update ten times faster at 3 × 10⁻⁴. This
follows Schwaller et al. (2021), who find the initial learning rate the most consequential
hyperparameter for this class of model, with the useful range between 1 × 10⁻⁵ and 1 × 10⁻⁴.
Smooth L1 loss, batch 16, six epochs per fold.

**Fifteen independent fine-tuning runs** — all five folds of all three schemes — each starting
from the pretrained weights rather than inheriting the previous fold's adaptation. The earlier
pipeline fine-tuned a single fold for time-budget reasons and reported the result alongside
five-fold baselines. Removing that discrepancy is one of the things this study exists to do,
and it cost the compute to do it.

### 2.6 Baseline

The training-fold mean. Scores −0.000 row-wise, −0.014 grouped, −0.071 scaffold. Present so
that every other number has a floor to be read against, and so that a negative R² elsewhere
can be recognised for what it is.

## 3. Which model is the result

**Three models share the lead and no ranking separates them.**

Pairwise Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on out-of-fold absolute errors resolve the six leading
models into two tiers:

- **Tier 1** — XGBoost (0.464), random forest (0.461), H1 (0.451). No pair differs
  significantly: p = 0.201, 0.641, 0.725.
- **Tier 2** — gradient boosting (0.443), SVR (0.411), H2 (0.411). Likewise internally
  indistinguishable: p = 0.507, 0.480, 0.113.
- **Every cross-tier comparison** is significant at p < 0.001.

The 0.003 separating XGBoost from the random forest is smaller than the fold-to-fold standard
deviation of either (0.125 and 0.157). **Reporting it as a ranking would overstate what the
data supports.**

## 4. The served model

The configuration selected for deployment is the **random forest, 250 trees at maximum depth
31**, chosen by Optuna's tree-structured Parzen estimator over ten trials against the
**scaffold** partition — the most demanding of the three — with trees searched on [100, 500]
and depth on [4, 32]. Best scaffold R² 0.3991.
([`../metrics/classical_tuning.json`](../metrics/classical_tuning.json))

It was preferred over XGBoost despite XGBoost's nominally higher grouped score because the two
are statistically tied, and because the random forest's per-tree structure makes the TreeSHAP
explanation the service returns both exact and fast.

Its feature contract is [`../model/contract.json`](../model/contract.json); no fitted weights
are published here (see [`../model/README.md`](../model/README.md)).

> **The tuned figures are optimistic by an unmeasured margin.** Nested cross-validation was
> implemented but not executed — see [`METHODS.md`](METHODS.md) §5 and
> [`LIMITATIONS.md`](LIMITATIONS.md) §3. The untuned benchmark carries the comparative
> conclusions.

## 5. What the comparison establishes

**Tree ensembles outperform graph networks, the fine-tuned transformer and the feedforward
network in this data regime**, and the reason is the regime rather than any defect in the deep
architectures. The input is heterogeneous tabular data of modest volume; the descriptor set
already supplies the representation that the deep models would have to learn, and 8,359 rows
is not enough to learn it better.

Three observations sharpen that:

1. **The ordering is not stable across schemes.** A leaderboard built on the row-wise
   convention ranks the random forest first and the MLP fifth; the honest scheme ranks XGBoost
   first and the MLP eleventh. The convention chooses the model.
2. **Scaffold generalisation separates the families more sharply than grouping does.** The
   tree ensembles hold at 0.398 while the graph networks collapse towards zero. A model that
   is competitive on a new substitution pattern may be useless on a new ring system.
3. **Structure alone is not enough for any family.** The structure-only controls reach 0.167
   and 0.153. Whatever the architecture, a model that is not told the conditions cannot
   predict a quantity that depends on them.
